# Testing NAC and sirolimus in cell models of BUB1B-related MVA
## Proposed laboratory study

**Track 2 · zoul6 · 24 October 2026**

## Abstract

We propose testing whether an approved drug can reduce chromosome-segregation errors in a BUB1B disease model while preserving cell division and viable cell output. We prioritize acetylcysteine (NAC) for a conditional first experiment. Published studies measured fewer segregation errors in oxidative-stress models, but neither used the relevant BUB1B disease model. Sirolimus is a separate backup for a model with harmful mTORC1 activity and secretory signaling. Neither compound has demonstrated correction of the relevant inherited defect in the evidence reviewed.

We reviewed primary papers and official drug labels, linked each claim to its evidence, checked records and exposure calculations, and designed laboratory tests that could reject either hypothesis. The tests count division errors alongside arrest, death and viable daughter-cell output. Restoring gene function, comparing cells with the same genetic background, and matching the degree of growth arrest help distinguish correction from changes in population composition.

Exposure is a central limitation. NAC studies used 5 mM in aged mouse fibroblasts and 500 µM in Sod1-null mouse oocytes. Acute intravenous approval does not establish sustained reduced-drug exposure or chronic suitability. Sirolimus reduced secretory signaling in other models; an allele-dependent BUBR1 mouse result establishes a possible mechanism, not drug rescue. We deprioritize metformin because of its large nominal exposure gap and reject dasatinib as a corrective lead because cell elimination can imitate improvement.

This is a literature-grounded experimental proposal. No new cell, animal or clinical experiments were performed. Advancement requires measured plausible exposure, reproducible functional benefit and no increase in genomic instability. No candidate has met those requirements, and this report makes no treatment recommendation.

## 1. Biological question and evidence

BUB1B encodes BubR1, which contributes to checkpoint signaling and kinetochore–microtubule attachment. Human disease-cell complementation studies show both abundance-sensitive and intrinsic functional defects. Raising protein abundance therefore cannot be assumed to restore function. The genetic hypothesis remains conditional on variant phase and residual function. Track 1 is separate; this analysis contains no individual sequence, phenotype or identifier. [M01](https://doi.org/10.1158/0008-5472.CAN-09-4319)

Mouse alleles can cause different pathology and mTORC1 activity despite similar BubR1 abundance. Each experimental model must establish its own defect. A correction to the relevant mouse paper concerns a lymphoma image and method; this proposal uses the separate muscle-signaling observations. [M02](https://www.jci.org/articles/view/126863), [correction](https://www.jci.org/articles/view/144781)

The central question is whether a reversible stress component worsens segregation or tissue function. A lower fraction of abnormal cells is insufficient evidence: the drug may have stopped their division or killed them.

## 2. Candidate priorities

| Priority | Compound | Proposed question | Evidence limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| First conditional test | Acetylcysteine (NAC) | Does reducing oxidative or replication stress reduce new segregation errors? | Mechanism and exposure remain unresolved; checkpoint restoration is unproven |
| Separate backup | Sirolimus | Does reducing harmful mTORC1/secretory activity preserve tissue function? | Downstream compensation is unproven in the relevant model |
| Deprioritized lead | Metformin | Can IKK/NF-κB-associated secretory activity be suppressed at plausible exposure? | Large nominal exposure gap; no disease-specific segregation rescue |
| Restricted comparator | Dasatinib | Does selective elimination change population composition? | Rejected as a corrective lead; elimination is not repair |

The order reflects the information a laboratory experiment could provide. It does not rank clinical efficacy. No combination is proposed: complementary pathway names do not establish added benefit or acceptable interactions.

### Acetylcysteine

Chen et al. measured fewer segregation errors after 5 mM NAC for 48 hours in aged mouse fibroblasts. A separate study reported benefit at 500 µM in Sod1-null mouse oocytes; its detailed exposure timing was not independently verified. Neither is a BUB1B disease model. Another oxidative-stress study linked NAC to preserved checkpoint-protein abundance, without establishing repair of an inherited defect. [C01](https://journals.biologists.com/jcs/article/136/11/jcs260688/316611/Oxidative-stress-induces-chromosomal-instability), [P13](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39845481/), [C02](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3799279/)

These results support testing a specific hypothesis: BUB1B dysfunction may leave a removable, NAC-sensitive stress component. That connection is inferred. Whether plausible sustained exposure preserves viable lineages with fewer errors is unknown. NAC is not assumed to replace missing BubR1 function. Normal redox measurements with persistent segregation errors would reject the proposed corrective mechanism.

Acetadote establishes approval for acute acetaminophen poisoning. It does not establish an interchangeable oral product, a chromosome-instability indication or chronic suitability. Historical acute-IV data reported a transient mean total-plasma peak of 554 mg/L and about 35 mg/L at 12 hours: approximately 3.39 mM and 0.214 mM. These observations are not proposed targets. The primary abstract associates adverse reactions with early high concentrations; the peak is not a tolerability benchmark. The label warns about hypersensitivity and fluid overload. Total, reduced and free NAC are different analytes, and route-dependent bioavailability prevents a simple culture-to-human conversion. [P11](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2025/021539s021lbl.pdf), [P14](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2598989/), [P15](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01061422)

### Sirolimus

Rapamycin at nominal 12.5 nM reduced IL1A-associated signaling in the senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP) of human fibroblasts. It did not reverse growth arrest. The BUBR1 mouse study found allele-specific muscle mTORC1 activation and discussed inhibition; it did not demonstrate rapamycin rescue. [P02](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncb3195), [M02](https://www.jci.org/articles/view/126863)

The proposed connection joins two observations from different models: increased mTORC1 in selected mouse muscle and reduced SASP after sirolimus elsewhere. Less tissue dysfunction is an inference; safe, sustained benefit is unknown. This branch fails if the relevant model lacks abnormal mTORC1 or a demonstrably harmful secretory state. Lower cytokines alone do not establish rescue.

Rapamune is approved for specified transplant prophylaxis and sporadic lymphangioleiomyomatosis. Adult transplant whole-blood concentrations cannot establish unbound tissue exposure. Blood partitioning, protein binding and co-medications matter. Infection, immunosuppression and impaired healing remain material liabilities. [P03](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/product-information/rapamune-epar-product-information_en.pdf), [P04, archived binding reference](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2020/021083s067,021110s085lbl.pdf)

### Why the alternatives are not leads

Metformin suppressed SASP-associated transcripts at 1–5 mM in culture. Usual labeled steady-state plasma concentrations are below approximately 7.7 µM after conversion using free-base molecular weight. The nominal gap exceeds about 129–646-fold. Intracellular accumulation was not measured and cannot establish an exposure bridge; this does not prove that accumulation is impossible. Mouse longevity findings at different exposure do not resolve the gap. [P06](https://www.mcgill.ca/pollak-lab/files/pollak-lab/metformin_inhibits_the_senescence-associated_secretory_phenotype_by_interfering_with_ikk.pdf), [P05](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=ec3a9070-3745-41fb-b610-8d06129d8fd9), [P07](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3736576/)

Dasatinib reduced viability in selected senescent cell types. That measures elimination. Genetic INK-ATTAC clearance in BubR1 mice provides disease context, but it is not evidence for dasatinib monotherapy. Marrow, bleeding, pulmonary and growth liabilities reinforce its rejection as a corrective lead. [P09](https://doi.org/10.1111/acel.12344), [M07](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature10600), [P08](https://packageinserts.bms.com/pi/pi_sprycel.pdf)

Other directions remain controls or exclusions. NMN-mediated protein abundance is not NMN lifespan rescue, and market approval was not verified. UMK57 is a research compound with context-dependent segregation effects, rather than an eligible approved lead. Increased abundance under proteasome blockade does not establish repair; HSP90 inhibition can worsen mutant instability. Tissue-specific and developmental counterevidence also cautions against blocking p53-related stress responses. Preserved function and genomic integrity must be measured together. [M05](https://doi.org/10.15252/embj.201386907), [P10](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7202060/), [M01](https://doi.org/10.1158/0008-5472.CAN-09-4319), [M08](https://doi.org/10.3389/fcell.2023.1282182), [S03](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2013.03.028)

## 3. Exposure comparisons

| Compound | Published assay concentration | Human comparison | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| NAC | 5,000 µM for 48 h; separate 500 µM oocyte result | Historical acute-IV total plasma: ~3,395 µM early peak; ~214 µM at 12 h | 5 mM is ~1.47× the early and ~23.3× the later value; sustained reduced/free tissue exposure is unknown |
| Sirolimus | 0.0125 µM nominal | 15 ng/mL whole blood ≈0.0164 µM in adult transplant context | Apparent overlap does not resolve differences in matrix, binding or duration |
| Metformin | 1,000–5,000 µM | Usual total plasma <7.74 µM | >129–646× benchmark gap; intracellular exposure unmeasured |
| Dasatinib | ≥0.05 µM nominal for three days | Total peak ≈0.168 µM; estimated free peak ~0.0067 µM | Free concentration is an estimate; transient peak and tissue exposure do not match the assay |

These comparisons are not directly interchangeable. None estimates efficacy, a safe dose or treatment probability. Calculations are reproducible in `analysis/build_ledger.py` and `analysis/generated/exposure_checks.json`; supporting evidence and limitations are recorded in P02–P15 and C01.

## 4. Proposed laboratory tests

### A. Establish a rescuable defect

Use cells with the same genetic background (isogenic controls) to compare BUB1B perturbation and correction. Add the normal gene as a rescue control, and compare variants expressed at matched protein abundance. Measure protein localization, checkpoint initiation and exit, attachment behavior and errors per division separately. Establish phase and transcript fate. Generic model development can begin without new patient material.

A technically validated genetic rescue at physiological expression must improve the attributed defect before a BUB1B-specific drug interpretation is justified. Reproducible partial rescue is informative; complete normalization is not required.

### B. Test the premise for each candidate

For NAC, measure oxidative and replication stress using assays based on different measurement principles, with medium-pH and chemical-interference controls. Specify oxygen conditions, passage and normal/young or isogenic-corrected controls because the published effect depended on context.

For sirolimus, establish mTORC1 engagement and harmful conditioned-medium activity. Normalize medium collection to viable donor-cell number and collection time. Measure or bound residual drug, include a matched residual-drug spike-in control, and use washout or removal where feasible. These controls distinguish a donor-secretory effect from direct drug exposure in recipient cells.

Include vehicle, isogenic rescue, an orthogonal pathway perturbation and matched growth arrest. Test monotherapies first. Stop any branch whose proposed mechanism is absent.

### C. Measure exposure, division and cell fate

Measure unbound drug in the medium and relevant intracellular analytes across a justified concentration and time range, including washout. Separate high-concentration mechanistic controls from concentrations considered for translation.

Enroll all observed mitotic entries in blinded live imaging. Record normal completion, erroneous completion, slippage, persistent arrest, death and loss to follow-up. Define observation windows and censoring rules in advance. Report errors per evaluable anaphase together with division frequency, mitotic duration and successful viable-lineage output per starting cell or mitotic entry.

Track both daughters, fixed-time outcomes and recovery after washout. Add matched population doublings and absolute euploid/aneuploid-cell counts; these do not replace complete fate accounting. A corrective effect must exceed an arrest/death-matched control. Downstream compensation must improve tissue-relevant function without increasing segregation errors or depleting healthy progenitors.

### D. Require replication and clear decisions

Use a pilot to estimate baseline variability and assay feasibility. Before confirmatory testing, fix the primary endpoint, minimum meaningful effect, power calculation and exclusion criteria. Also specify acceptable losses of viable-lineage/progenitor output, increases in failed mitoses and censoring imbalance. Define decisions using the uncertainty around these estimates.

An independent culture or model preparation is a biological replicate; each cell in a field is not. Randomize treatment allocation and analysis order, blind scoring, and report effect sizes with uncertainty. Identify exploratory secondary endpoints explicitly.

Advance only when independent preparations reproduce meaningful benefit at measured plausible exposure and orthogonal assays support the mechanism. Stop for worsening instability, impaired viable output, improvement explained only by selection, or an exposure mismatch. A nonsignificant harm test is not evidence of safety. An encouraging efficacy estimate with uninformative safety intervals remains insufficient.

These experiments have not been performed. Short cell assays cannot establish developmental reversibility, tissue-wide benefit or long-term tumor consequences. Suppressing stress or preserving an abnormal cell may have competing long-term effects.

## 5. What would change the conclusion?

Removing C01 removes the somatic NAC segregation evidence; the separate meiotic study cannot replace a mitotic BUB1B model. Removing P02 removes the principal sirolimus secretory-modulation experiment. Allele-associated mTORC1 alone cannot establish benefit. Removing M01 weakens the human functional-rescue rationale. The analysis checks the consequences of removing individual sources and whole study families; citation counts are not treated as evidence strength.

Drop NAC if oxidative stress is absent. Drop sirolimus if either abnormal mTORC1 or harmful secretory activity is absent. If functional BubR1 is effectively absent, neither hypothesis can be presented as replacing checkpoint function. If the causal gene premise fails, rebuild the model.

Do not advance while the free-drug, analyte, tissue or duration comparison remains unresolved. Stop translation if the required measured exposure exceeds a justified feasible and safe range, even when the cell phenotype improves. These are conditional decisions, not calibrated response probabilities.

## 6. Methods, reproducibility and contribution

The literature search was bounded and purposive rather than systematic. We reviewed primary papers and official labels using public gene, pathway and drug terms. Evidence records identify whether full text, abstracts or excerpts were available. Duplicate citations and database mirrors belong to the same study family. Approval of nominated compounds was checked against regulator/manufacturer labeling. Organizer guidance permits justified approved-drug combinations; investigational status alone does not establish eligibility. [R01](https://huggingface.co/spaces/SageBio/rare-disease-real-kid-mva-hackathon-2026/discussions/5)

zoul6 reviewed the report and supplied scientific judgments and wording input. OpenAI Codex assisted source discovery, drafting, coding and AI review. Four AI review roles challenged the analysis from different perspectives. Their agreement is not independent biological replication, clinical expertise or a calibrated probability. The work used public-only queries and a privacy-minimized brief; controlled dataset records were not accessed for this Track 2 analysis.

zoul6 reports using OpenAI ChatGPT Pro with Codex, with model training disabled and no thumbs-up or thumbs-down feedback supplied on sensitive project chats. These are user-reported facts, not an independent settings audit or a certification of all privacy compliance.

The supporting package contains public-source records, 14 causal links with their direction of effect, four compound dispositions, seven exposure comparisons, source extracts from AI-assisted review and offline Python checks. Source extractions support reproduction of the evidence records. Original review records and the targeted NAC addendum are retained separately. Input hashes identify the files checked. Repeatable checks examine source removal, study-family removal and alternative mechanisms.

The code checks consistency of records and calculations; it cannot establish whether a paper is true. It performs no docking, predictor training, efficacy simulation or patient-sequence analysis. The contribution is a testable decision process: it identifies a useful next experiment, specifies what would disprove the rationale and guards against mistaking arrest or cell loss for repair. Reusable evidence records and experimental criteria may help avoid uninformative screens. Biological benefit remains untested.
